\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This dissertation studies the Bitcoin volatility risk premium (BVRP), the
difference between the expected volatility over the following 30 days and
the implied volatility of Deribit's DVOL, using daily data from March
2021 to March 2026. Measured with realized volatility, the premium is negative
on average ($-8.76$ percentage points): implied volatility typically exceeds
subsequent realized volatility.
Linear and machine learning models have lower out-of-sample errors than the
historical mean in the expanding window, but the model confidence set does not
separate them from it. We find no evidence that the variables available at $t$
predict Bitcoin log returns over 1 to 60 days, either with a linear model or with a random forest. The Ridge-forecast
BVRP precedes lower returns, but the evidence is weak and fragile, limited to
the one-day horizon. With few independent observations, the lack of rejection
is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence of predictability.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Bitcoin; volatility risk premium; implied volatility; DVOL; return predictability; machine learning.
