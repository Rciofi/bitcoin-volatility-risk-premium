\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This dissertation provides a comprehensive investigation of the \textit{Bitcoin
Volatility Risk Premium} (BVRP), defined as the difference between implied and
realized volatility. The objective is to characterize its statistical
properties, identify volatility regimes, and evaluate its predictive ability
for future Bitcoin returns. The 30-day implied volatility (IV30D) is obtained
from the Deribit Volatility Index (DVOL), while realized volatility (RV30D) is
constructed using daily log returns of Bitcoin spot prices.

Empirical evidence shows that the BVRP is positive on average, indicating a
persistent protection premium in the Bitcoin options market, consistent with
the broader volatility risk premium literature. The distribution of the BVRP
exhibits marked negative skewness and heavy tails, reflecting stress episodes
driven by sharp spikes in realized volatility. The time-series dynamics reveal
distinct regimes—including complacency, transition, and stress—highlighting the
structural sensitivity of Bitcoin volatility to market shocks.

The relationship between the BVRP and future returns is moderate but becomes
more pronounced at longer horizons, particularly at 20 days. To further assess
this predictive structure, nonlinear machine learning models (Random Forest and
XGBoost) are implemented. The results indicate that volatility-related
variables—especially RV30D and the BVRP itself—carry incremental information
about future returns, although the overall predictive power remains moderate due
to the inherently noisy nature of cryptocurrency markets.

The findings suggest that the BVRP is more effective as a regime indicator than
as an isolated trading signal, offering value when integrated into hybrid
quantitative models, risk filters, and volatility-based strategies. While not a
standalone predictor, the BVRP contributes meaningfully to the characterization
of market conditions and to frameworks for tactical decision-making.

\textbf{Keywords:} Bitcoin; implied volatility; realized volatility; volatility
risk premium; machine learning; DVOL; Deribit.
